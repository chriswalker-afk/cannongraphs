# PR14 — Quadratic cannons

The exact rational polynomial parser now supports genuine degree-two expressions for purple quadratic cannons, including expanded, factorised and completed-square forms, fractions and Unicode x². Cancelling the quadratic term is rejected by a quadratic cannon; straight-line cannons keep their previous rules. Both families are supported in one immutable route plan. A missing/wrong family prevents launching, whereas a valid equation that misses its firing anchor still consumes one Pip and uses the disappointed misfire animation.

The quadratic segment is parameterised by x in the firing direction; y is evaluated on the submitted polynomial, without snapping to cannon or exit artwork. Capture uses the existing numerical point tolerances. The first world-edge intersection, exact-coordinate capture or solid body contact ends a leg. Solid contact wins a capture tie. For rectangular terrain, partition the parameter interval at the rectangle's x/y faces, then find the critical points of the piecewise squared distance (degree at most four). Derivative roots and bisection isolate the earliest continuous contact, including tangencies and thin obstacles, with 1e-9 world-unit numerical contact tolerance. The same extrema supply authoring clearance audits. Standard floating-point arithmetic is used for geometry; extremely ill-conditioned expressions can lose numerical precision. Animation duration uses a 64-segment arc-length estimate, not a collision sampling grid. Flight progress is linear in x, not constant arc-length speed.

Cannon angle comes from the derivative at the launch anchor; airborne Pips follow the changing tangent. The attempted trace and rapid-fire trails are exact quadratic Bezier paths under the affine camera. No graph is drawn in planning. Reduced motion, camera controls, tested-leg status, five-Pip budget, celebrations and scoring remain compatible. The shared equation editor shows the appropriate family, has an x² insertion key and renders exact coefficients using MathML. Quadratic feedback uses substitution, turning points and curvature rather than treating an entire parabola as a single gradient. No reference answer is exposed.

A provisional third-world preview is selectable after the two completed linear worlds; it combines a quadratic arch and a linear relay. It is an engine demonstration, not the final World 3 sequence, and does not force a unique quadratic or a particular route. PR15 replaces this preview with the full Parabola Peaks curriculum. Existing 24 linear levels remain unchanged.
