# PR35 — Rebalanced quadratic worlds

The first three levels introduce a single graph, the next three use two cannons, the next three use three and the final three use four. All levels remain selectable in Cadet, Pilot and Ace. The displayed level number is its catalogue position, not the stable puzzle ID.

Parabola Peaks retains the three single-cannon introductions, the two-curve Arch and valley and the four-cannon Summit gauntlet. Rainbow relay now has an additional gate so it cannot be bypassed; it gets a new ID rather than inheriting records. Roots of the ravine is extended with a linear leg; translated squares, multiple roots, mixed opening directions, unequal endpoint heights and turning points reappear through the new relay sequence.

Lost Arches keeps two fractional-scale introductions and the fractional-root puzzle, followed by translated curves, leftward flights, mixed families and switchbacks. The original Temple relay and Labyrinth switchback remain unchanged. The former five-cannon Vault of five is retired from the regular sequence; Vault of four and Temple switchback are fresh four-cannon puzzles, not identical replacements with the same progress key. Optional bonus finales are a future design decision.

Five puzzles per world remain unchanged (same geometry, title, ID, gems). Fourteen new or reworked puzzles have r-prefixed IDs. Existing achievement retention from PR34 protects previously earned cosmetic rewards. Newly authored levels have no inherited completion record.

All reference equations stay in tests. The route audit checks all original engine rules, each cannon in sequence, continuous collision clearance, collection of every authored gem, support footprints and waiting crew. A conservative interval proof uses actual solid geometry slices to exclude quadratic shortcuts without assuming a unique coefficient; linear shortcuts use exact simulation. Alternative valid equations remain accepted. No forced answer, extra collision mechanic or forced route sequence is added to the game. Classroom difficulty and visual review are separate from automated checks.
