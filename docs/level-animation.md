# PR29 — Bring the home-screen animation into levels

The home screen already had a bouncing launch crew and a visible rapid-fire stream; the playable graph showed the crew only in its header. Levels now stage waiting Pips on available surfaces in the actual scene, close to the first cannon where possible. No new terrain, false coordinate marks or obstacle geometry are added. The scene updates the waiting crew from the real attempt state: dispatch removes a Pip, a failed Cadet flight replenishes it, and successful rapid fire drains the waiting queue. The header remains the authoritative accessible count. For unusually crowded future layouts the scene may omit characters rather than obscure mathematical targets.

The first flight uses the existing Pip expressions, cannon charge/recoil/burst, exact route and terminal animations, with extra launch squash/stretch and a short warm trajectory trail matching the home demonstration. When the nearby boarding hop is clear, the next waiting Pip hops towards the breech; otherwise the established local loading cue is used. The pre-fire hop is decorative, not a mathematical flight. The sprite's centre during flight stays on the student's actual equation, not a guessed or smoothed curve. No preview before launch in Pilot/Ace. Failed attempts still show the correct misfire or splat rather than a successful launch.

Normal/reduced animation can be explicitly selected in the level toolbar (system preference initially, session override carried to subsequent levels). The choice is fixed while a flight or celebration is running. Reduced motion keeps the static crew and semantic result without the moving trail or recoil. Motion is never enabled against the system preference without an explicit student choice. Home pause/play remains local to its decorative demo.

Tests cover crew placement, depletion, boarding clearance, full-motion trajectory samples and reduced motion. Production Chromium coverage now exercises actual normal-motion in-game flight and rapid-fire frames, failure animations, and the full/reduced switch in addition to existing reduced-motion classroom tests. Real classroom playtesting and subjective animation polish remain outstanding.
