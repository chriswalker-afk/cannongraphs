Objectives guide the intended approach, but two anchors do not uniquely determine a quadratic. Terrain constraints introduce curvature, height and turning-point reasoning without requiring an exact stored answer or particular algebraic representation. All mathematically valid successful routes remain accepted. Reference equations and expected sequences are confined to tests. Individual clearance audits ensure positive body clearance; alternate valid solutions and incorrect curvatures are tested. In the final two relays, equal-height anchors and high/low obstacles constrain shortcuts: a quadratic joining two equal-height endpoints has one sign of displacement from that height in the interior. Skipping across an arch and valley therefore cannot pass both. The last arch-to-exit shortcut in the finale also has equal heights at the centers of the high wall and low gate (x=16,22), making it impossible. Sampled rational shortcut tests supplement this geometric reasoning.

New original alpine scenery features distant snow-capped peaks, lilac stone and evergreen silhouettes. These are decorative; terrain remains exact rectangular geometry. The current engine uses x-monotone quadratic functions and existing numeric tolerances, not physical projectile dynamics. Manual browser QA remains outstanding: play all twelve levels, inspect axes and gates, evaluate the new theme at small screen sizes, check the four-cannon finale and reduced-motion celebration.
