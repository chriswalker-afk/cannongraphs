# PR49 — Normal and broken cannon categories

Play opens the ordinary world chooser with a two-position Normal cannons / Broken cannons segmented switch. Normal retains the familiar six cards in a 3×2 desktop arrangement. Broken contains the single Broken Foundry card, and future worlds are classified by authored lift metadata rather than their names. Category and selected level remain consistent on back-navigation; both categories share learning-mode selection, the standard four-tier numbered level selector and completion records. Session category is remembered on returning home. Keyboard arrows, Home/End and touch operate the switch. The homepage no longer has the extra flight-test button; optional unranked lift demos are accessible from the broken-world chooser.

Broken Foundry has a distinctive amber steelworks postcard with two striped lift decks, cracked barrels, locks, grounded rails, a Pip and a decorative illustrative route. The in-game foundry scene uses low-contrast gantries, arched windows, furnace silhouettes and pipes, varying by puzzle band. Terrain rectangles retain exact collision edges, with foundry-specific rivets, vents and striped caps. Lift rails are visually braced to the real floor/terrain, while the actual rail stops and muzzle coordinates remain unchanged. The workshop shares deck and lock art. Graph-family colours and exact axes remain legible.

The existing twelve level IDs, cannon/exit coordinates, locked coefficients, rail ranges and mathematical constraints remain unchanged. Unused right-hand margins are cropped to one unit beyond the exit; quadratic levels remove two unused units above the highest rails and ceilings end at the revised boundary. This changes the playable bounding rectangle, not the intended route. Full uniqueness, clearance, crew staging and curriculum regression tests must validate the revised bounds. No progress reset or new puzzle IDs.

Browser coverage checks navigation, category counts, keyboard switching, no homepage shortcut, all twelve Foundry scenes, retained selection and narrow/landscape/desktop layouts. Actual visual review on classroom hardware remains necessary.
